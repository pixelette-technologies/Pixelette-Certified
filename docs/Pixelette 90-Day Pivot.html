01 · The hard truth

What 1.5 people and £5,000 cannot buy

Three things have to be said before the plan, because the plan only works if they are accepted.

1. You cannot build three products. You can build one and document two.

Only one of your three offers is software. The other two are methodology products: a diagnostic method and a governance artefact pack. They are documents, templates and a repeatable process, not code. That is not a compromise, it is the correct answer at your scale, and it is why they can be sellable in weeks rather than quarters. The mistake would be treating all three as engineering builds.

2. ISO 42001 certification is not happening this year.

Audit fees alone for an organisation of 126 to 175 employees run £9,000 to £17,500 for initial certification, before consultancy, remediation or platform costs, on a three to nine month timeline. That is two to three and a half times your entire six-month budget. What to do instead Buy the standard, build and publish the artefacts, get a gap-assessment quote from a UKAS-accredited body, and say exactly that. Total cash: about £600. Never imply certification you do not hold; enterprise buyers check accredited registers.

3. Grant money will not arrive inside 90 days.

Innovate UK BridgeAI ended June 2026. The Flexible AI Upskilling Fund closed May 2024. Every Innovate UK AI competition checked is closed. UK Shared Prosperity Fund closed March 2026, taking many growth hubs with it. TechLocal closed to applications in March 2026. Do not build a 90-day plan around grant income. Two exceptions survive and both are in the plan: Skills Bootcamps at 10% SME contribution, and fully funded apprenticeships for 16 to 24 year olds.

What that leaves, and it is more than it sounds

You are not starting from nothing. You hold two certified management systems, seven years of delivered work, named clients, quantified case studies and a delivery network. The gap is AI-specific proof and AI-specific method, and the research says that is exactly the gap buyers weigh most heavily.

The finding that should shape everything you do next

A survey of 310 enterprise buyers across 266 companies, published 18 August 2026, asked what evidence they prioritise when evaluating AI suppliers. The answer: proven integration 47%, measurable business outcomes 32%, transparency 23%. Buyers demand "specific problem resolution through comparable client case studies paired with quantified financial returns, not merely showcase AI capabilities or roadmaps."

Not one of the top three is a certification. One published case study with a real integration and a real number outranks a wall of badges. That is unusually good news for a firm with 1.5 FTE and £5,000, because the binding constraint becomes a client saying yes rather than a budget you do not have.

Why this is defensible rather than a shortcut

"We run our own operations this way, here is the measurement, here are the artefacts, here is the monitoring dashboard" is a stronger opening than any certificate, and it is the one proof asset that costs nothing and cannot be disputed. It also forces the honest version of the methodology, because you feel the parts that do not work. Say plainly that the first case study is internal. Buyers respect it; pretending otherwise is what damages credibility.

The constraint to respect: pick processes that matter and that you would fix anyway. Proposal production, bid qualification, recruitment screening, case study drafting, client reporting. If the internal work is theatre, the case study will read as theatre.

The licence trap that would have cost you the business

Arize Phoenix is Elastic License 2.0, which forbids exactly this model. Verbatim: "You may not provide the software to third parties as a hosted or managed service, where the service provides users with access to any substantial set of the features or functionality of the software." Phoenix is fine deployed inside the client's own cloud account, where the client is the licensee. It is not fine as your multi-tenant platform.

Langfuse core is MIT and safe. Its /ee directories are separately licensed and forbid resale, but those cover only project-level RBAC, audit logs, data masking and similar. Use one Langfuse organisation per client rather than project-level roles and you stay entirely inside MIT. Grafana is AGPLv3: ship it unmodified and brand through provisioned dashboards, not forked source, and it costs you nothing.

07 · Credibility

Five moves that cost almost nothing

1. AI DPS RM6200 · free, week 1

Registration is free with no cap on suppliers, continuous entry, and the agreement runs to 23 February 2029. Entry is a self-declaration across four filter levels plus a Data Ethics Letter of Understanding and evidence you already hold: ISO 9001, ISO 27001, insurance. That signed ethics undertaking is also a free credibility artefact you can cite in private-sector bids. Note Crown Commercial Service became the Government Commercial Agency on 1 April 2026.

2. Claude Partner Network Select · ~£800

Requirements formalised 3 June 2026: 10 certified individuals, 2 production deployments within 12 months, 1 public customer story. No revenue commitment, no annual designation fee. Compare Microsoft's Solutions Partner designation at $4,875 a year before you clear any Azure consumption threshold. The catch is real though: certifications belong to individuals and partners must maintain certified people who have used Claude within the past 90 days. Choose people who will actually use it.

3. A published methodology · free

The highest value per pound available. Publish how you scope, evaluate, handle model risk, set acceptance criteria and what you refuse to do, grounded in NIST AI RMF and its Generative AI Profile. Cite Anthropic, OpenAI, NIST, OWASP and UK AISI rather than inventing. Publishing a methodology that cites rather than invents is itself the credibility signal.

4. A contribution to UK AISI's inspect_evals · free

MIT licensed, maintained by the UK AI Security Institute with Arcadia Impact and the Vector Institute. Permanently and publicly attributed, objective acceptance bar, submission simplified on 8 May 2026. "We maintain an evaluation in the UK AI Security Institute's Inspect Evals repository" is a stronger sentence than any marketing copy, and for a UK firm selling assurance to UK buyers it is worth more than a logo.

5. The AI Assurance Consortium orbit · free

Launched from the September 2025 Trusted Third-Party AI Assurance Roadmap, chaired by BCS with UKAS, BSI, NPL and the Ada Lovelace Institute, industry advisory group led by techUK. It is building a voluntary code of ethics and a skills and competencies framework right now. Your existing ISO 9001 and 27001 are exactly what makes you a plausible early participant rather than a newcomer. Start at the free 28 September event.

The proof asset that beats all five

One case study with a real integration and a real number. 47% of enterprise buyers rank proven integration first and 32% rank measurable outcomes second. Certification does not appear in the top three. Your internal Pixelette baseline delivers this in week 6 with no client sign-off to wait for.

The four-person precedent worth studying

Parlance Labs is a four-person team. Its founder noticed he was repeating the same advice, wrote it down publicly for free, taught a course to over 3,500 students from Fortune 500 companies and major AI labs including OpenAI, Meta and Google, and now qualifies clients out, directing those without baseline knowledge to the free resources first. Note what he says about open source specifically: his contributions have diminished because it is now easier to build your own tools with AI. The writing, not the repository, was the asset. That is the entire escalation ladder, executed by four people: free artefact, then course, then book, then inbound consulting.
